## Wet fin and coil model

The selected 1 C temperature is now the **two fin-root temperatures**, not the temperature of the whole fin. Both top and bottom fin roots are assumed perfectly connected to cooling. Solve half the 17.78 mm height with an insulated symmetry plane. Constant conductivity, 12 conduction intervals and negligible through-thickness temperature gradient are assumed.

The steady equation per unit channel length is `k*t*T'' + 2*h*(Tgas-T) + 2*hfg*rho_dry*km*max(wgas-wsat(T),0) = 0`. A tridiagonal Newton iteration solves the temperature-dependent saturation term. Vapor condensation stops locally where its driving difference is negative; no artificial condensation is imposed on a dry region. The root remains 1 C. Sensible and mass efficiencies are calculated separately from the solved temperature profile, then weighted with the isothermal top/bottom channel base areas. Report both dry analytical efficiency and wet total-heat fin efficiency.

Inlet gas conditions set these effective efficiencies. Along-channel changes in fin temperature, film properties and wetting are not resolved, and the fin-root cooling connection itself is still assumed. Fin efficiency therefore improves the previous isothermal-wall model without validating the physical cooling assembly.

Mass-transfer area uses `A = -Q/(km*eta_mass)*log(1-removal/maximum_removal)`. Outlet temperature uses the sensible NTU with h*eta_heat. Cooling is moist-gas enthalpy loss minus liquid enthalpy at the root temperature. Heat duty is thermal load, not refrigeration electrical input. Gas volume and properties remain fixed at inlet values for each interval.

If this simplified coil model predicts bulk outlet RH above 100%, its wall-transfer area and formal thermal duty are retained as **provisional requirements**, with the design flagged invalid for a demonstrated vapor-only outlet. Fog transport/capture is not assumed or silently credited. Such a flag does not prove the proposed hardware can never work; it identifies physics that must be validated before accepting its size.

Straight-channel Darcy friction is calculated from the rectangular Poiseuille relation. It excludes entrances, offsets, headers and wetting/flooding losses. Re >= 2300 is rejected. The entry-length warning remains a correlation-domain diagnostic, without an area reduction.

## Full EVA sizing

The in-memory `EVA_Profile` contains all 960 half-minute intervals of the current eight-hour EVA, with physical and FOS-adjusted net vapor, pressure, dry oxygen flow, supply humidity ratio and regional mean/outlet air states. Interval states are coincident; independent peaks are not combined. The source is the existing net vapor reaching PLSS after the original suit condensation/re-evaporation calculation. Mars metabolic adjustment is not applied to moisture. FOS is included once.

**Peak case:** calculate area and thermal duty for every interval at its original physical inlet state and FOS-adjusted water requirement. Maximum required area sets the channel count; maximum thermal duty sets refrigeration capacity; maximum water rate sets the separator and extraction pump. These maxima can occur in different intervals. Average thermal power uses the time integral, and water storage uses integrated generated water, not peak rate times eight hours. The original air profile is not a 65%-RH-controlled profile.

**65% case:** integrate three serial finite gas inventories, representing neck, torso and legs. Dry oxygen circulates from the condenser to neck, torso, legs and back. Regional net moisture sources are reconstructed from the original JOS-3 outlet humidity increments and scaled by FOS. The original node temperatures are prescribed. Equal node gas volumes and fixed reference dry-gas inventories are assumed; initial bulk RH is 50%. The condenser runs continuously, without active humidification. Check RH at every modelled regional air node, supply and final return.

For each node, `M_dry*dw/dt = mdot_dry*(w_in-w) + source`. Step size is at most 1 s and 0.2 of the smallest node residence time. The condenser mass effectiveness is based on its fin-corrected area. Fin/transport coefficients for this transient case are evaluated once per interval at the 65% return reference state, rather than recomputed every substep.

First test **ideal infinite area and a saturated 1 C return from the condenser**. This is a favorable sink bound independent of fin efficiency. If it already exceeds 65%, do not invent a finite area. If it meets 65%, bracket and bisect area and rerun the complete humidity history to identify a finite size. Any retained vapor changes actual collected liquid through the transient mass balance.

This is a one-way RH design study. It does not rerun JOS-3 evaporation, sweat retention, air heating or wall condensation as humidity changes. It checks modelled bulk-air nodes, not humidity inside the garment or every physical pocket in the suit. Predictions after a 65% breach are not accepted operating states; the plotted ideal trace stops at the first breached interval, and an unconstrained supersaturation indicator is not reported as an actual humidity maximum.

## Weighted full-EVA outlet sizing (current; supersedes average/99% designs)
The selected design now minimises a user-weighted objective over whole condenser channels and a discrete grid of permitted minimum fin-root temperatures. Each root floor enumerates channels upwards to the first passing system; the next smaller permitted count is therefore explicitly rejected. A lower-bound optimum is identified as bound-limited rather than proof about smaller excluded counts. The minimum-channel feasible result at every floor is retained for the trade curve. Larger counts at a fixed floor cannot improve this core-volume objective.

`SizeWeight` is restricted to [0,1]. Cost is `w*(V-Vmin)/(Vmax-Vmin) + (1-w)*(Tmax-Tfloor)/(Tmax-Tmin)`, using fixed editable channel/temperature bounds. V is the gas/fin core envelope, not the complete package or mass. Weight 1 selects the smallest feasible core; weight 0 selects the warmest feasible allowed floor, with minimum size breaking ties. Intermediate weights balance these normalised terms. Bounds affect the numerical weighting. This is a grid-constrained selection, not continuous/global optimisation or a refrigeration-power estimate. The controller can operate roots warmer than its chosen minimum floor.

Every accepted candidate covers all internal steps and interval endpoints, meeting final supply RH and temperature tolerances (defaults 0.5 percentage points and 0.1 C). No startup grace is hidden. Supply is after cooling and reheating, throughout the EVA. Non-condensing sensible cooling remains possible when the inlet is already at/below the target humidity ratio; water is never added. Dry air, inadequate sensible cooling, saturation at the condenser, the selected suit-RH ceiling, out-of-range channel flow, and inadequate warmer-water temperature/pinch all reject a candidate. No mist capture/transport or freezing physics is introduced. All-infeasible searches report reasons/times, invalidate the workbook design-status sheet and stop downstream sizing; previous workbook numerical snapshots are not current accepted hardware in that case.

Wet-fin response is cached per interval/root temperature and interpolated during ideal root control. Reference transfer coefficients still use the permitted return RH and prescribed JOS-3 sources/temperatures. Feedback to physiology and thermal controller dynamics are not modelled. The finite RH inventory and exact integrated water-balance check are retained.

Air-warmer transfer area and water flow use coincident internal-step/end-point cold temperatures, supply targets, humidity ratios and heating demands. Both the inventory and warmer property evaluations are covered. Maximum required Q/(U*LMTD) sets area; peak Q with the chosen water drop sets flow. Available warm-water temperature, positive counterflow approaches and laminar channel flow are checked. Selected hardware is assumed modulated/bypassed ideally at part load, without delay. Source-water availability and refrigeration hardware capacity are prescribed requirements, not independently modelled equipment.

Tank volume follows integrated collected water. Peak cooling, condensate removal and reheat requirements include interval endpoints. Sensitivity reruns the constrained design search for moisture load, supply temperature/RH, and endpoint size weights; body sources/temperatures remain prescribed. Figures show the feasible size-temperature frontier, selected point and maximum absolute within-interval supply deviations. Existing margins remain explicit; no 99% asymptotic-capacity sizing or extra cooling multiplier is used.

Validation: `tests/testEVARHSizing.m` exercises an analytical synthetic wet case, whole-channel minima, weight endpoints, target compliance, full-duration integration/water balance, dry-inlet rejection and unavailable-warmer rejection. MATLAB code-analyzer and runtime outcomes are reported separately; passing static analysis is not full physiological/hardware validation.

For subsequent physical modelling, the simplest extension is equilibrium condensation with a retained-liquid inventory at each gas node. Transfer excess vapour into liquid while conserving total water; either solve node temperature and phase change together via enthalpy conservation or explicitly account for latent heat removed by any prescribed-temperature boundary. Permit re-evaporation only up to available liquid and add drainage separately. This is consistent in form with the existing JOS-3 airNodeCondensation routine, but source bookkeeping must avoid counting its already-modelled condensate twice. Gas-node condensate, retained skin sweat and condenser-collected water must be distinct inventories.

Higher-fidelity alternatives are finite-rate wall/garment-film condensation with surface temperatures and mass-transfer coefficients, or a suspended-droplet phase with growth/evaporation, deposition and transport. COMSOL's moisture/heat transport documentation describes surface liquid inventory and coupled evaporation/condensation fluxes: https://doc.comsol.com/6.4/doc/com.comsol.help.models.heat.condensation_electronic_device_transport_diffusion/condensation_electronic_device_transport_diffusion.html . Droplet models require additional closures and data; surface-only vapour transport cannot resolve suspended fog. Pure-oxygen psychrometric/mass-transfer properties must be retained when adapting air-based models.


## Regional equilibrium condensation and runtime changes (current)
EquilibriumCondensation is now enabled by default in sizeEVARH and explicitly in the main script. Each suit-air outlet node retains its own additional liquid store, initially InitialRegionalLiquid_g = [0,0,0]. At each internal timestep (and when prescribed temperatures change), total local water is partitioned between vapor up to saturation and retained liquid; dry gas re-evaporates only the available liquid. Condensate does not drain, migrate between regions, or enter the condenser as droplets. Skin-retained sweat, previously modelled JOS-3 condensate, and condenser-collected water remain distinct. The regional source is already the net JOS-3 vapor source: only additional recirculation/margin-induced condensation is stored here; existing JOS-3 condensation is not added again.

This is an ISOTHERMAL equilibrium closure at the prescribed JOS-3 regional outlet temperatures, not an adiabatic gas-temperature solution. Latent heat is h_v(T)-h_l(T), with the existing vapor/constant liquid cp conventions. Positive RequiredPhaseHeatSink_W is the additional heat rejection needed to maintain the prescribed temperature; negative is heat input. Sensible heating/cooling of retained liquid when prescribed temperatures change is included. This requirement is reported separately and is not silently added to condenser cooling or LCG removal: supplying it requires an explicit thermal boundary/hardware integration not represented here. Vapor-plus-liquid-plus-condenser-collection mass balance and phase-vapor/liquid/boundary energy accounting have explicit residual checks. Full gas/body energy feedback is outside this closure. Freezing is rejected.

Selected summaries/workbook histories include final and peak local liquid inventory, gross condensation/re-evaporation, average phase heat requirement, raw pre-equilibrium supersaturation and equilibrium RH. Peak retained liquid is tracked at internal steps, not just reporting endpoints. Figure 10 shows per-region liquid, interval-average condensation/evaporation rates and the prescribed-temperature heat requirement. The existing storage tank is sized for condenser-collected water only, because regional liquid has no modelled drainage route. Cold-condenser supersaturation remains a rejection; suit liquid equilibrium does not add condenser fog physics.

Runtime changes preserve the channel enumeration, root-temperature resolution, integration timestep and acceptance tolerances. Saturation/latent properties are precomputed. Wet-fin response caching uses the exact return-temperature/pressure/dry-flow arrays, gas/water properties, geometry, response-grid size and property-function handle; histories and source-dependent states are never cached. Cache entries are bounded and invalidated when their inputs change; UseTransferCache=false provides a reference path. Candidate result rows are accumulated in a preallocated cell array and concatenated once. Out-of-correlation flows are rejected before integration. Feasible histories for each temperature floor are retained, allowing weight-only sensitivities to re-score the same frontier without rerunning physical simulations. Warmer property/area calculations are vectorised over coincident samples with unchanged equations. Runtime_s reports actual MATLAB search time when run; no measured speedup is claimed without an executable MATLAB run.

Tests include closed-form phase partitioning, evaporation limits, water and phase-energy conservation, temperature-change sensible heat, cache-on/off and cache invalidation, and reweight-versus-full-search equality. Standalone MATLAB code analysis and independent numerical reference checks do not replace running these integration tests in MATLAB.
