# RH physical component sizing: basis and limits

Run the merged `Nominal_Metabolic_Profile.m`, then run `RH_Component_Sizing.m`. The first runs the metabolic/RH calculations and writes `RH_Component_Inputs.xlsx`; the component script reads its labelled, unit-checked parameters. This remaining component handoff is a saved snapshot, so refresh the two programs in that order after changing inputs. The metabolic-to-RH transfer is now in memory.

The result is a provisional full-EVA peak hardware estimate, not a detailed build or a demonstrated RH controller. Peak refrigeration and liquid extraction capabilities are sized separately from mean electrical demand and integrated storage. A 20% cooling allowance is assumed. Average moisture already includes the upstream metabolic margin; storage and pumping do not apply that margin again. No external radiator hardware, battery mass, full PLSS enclosure, complete ventilation blower, external reheater or suit ducting is sized. RH heat is passed to shared water after the LCG and before external cooling. Added ventilation power for this condenser is included. Compressor on-state power is reported separately from mean power; motor-start transients are excluded.

## Calculations

- **Condenser:** rectangular fin volumes and two porous base plates, with an aluminium shell. Listed fin thickness determines solid volume. A 1 mm shell and 10 mm inlet/outlet header allowances are assumed. Casing is an envelope/mass allowance, not a completed pressure-housing stress analysis. The upstream model still assumes isothermal wetted fins; fin conduction/contact losses require later verification.
- **Separator:** round up the required liquid area to whole sheets using the core's planform. Stack height is sheet count times a 2 mm sheet/collection-space pitch. Mass is installed ceramic area times thickness times assumed bulk density. Plumbing supports are in the accessory allowance. No detailed porous-manifold flow distribution is solved.
- **Cold heat exchanger:** area is cooling duty divided by the assumed allowable footprint heat flux, with a lower bound equal to core footprint. A 4 mm plate, 60% metal volume and a 3 K evaporator approach are assumed. Plate volume is not added inside the porous-core envelope. Thermal spreading between the larger cold plate and the smaller porous core is idealized; the calculation does not independently prove uniform 1 C fins.
- **Refrigeration:** use enough reference compressors to meet margin-adjusted duty. Mean electrical input is reference input power times the ideal duty fraction; on-state input retains full reference input. Cycling losses and low-load control losses are not measured. The map's lowest condensing temperature, 100 F (37.78 C), is used by default; the cold side is surface temperature minus 3 K. Operation in a PLSS requires adaptation of oil return, compressor heat rejection, containment and controls. These are reference analogues rather than qualified installations.
- **Pump:** required flow is twice collected liquid volume rate; required pressure rise is separator suction plus 2 kPa downstream routing loss. The tank is pressure-balanced to suit pressure through a bladder. The reported inlet absolute pressure is suit pressure minus suction. Hydraulic power is pressure rise times required flow. Mean electrical input retains 1.44 W continuously rather than assuming that the reference free-flow rating persists under load. The reference pump's temperature range does not qualify 1 C water, nor do atmospheric suction tests establish operation at low absolute pressure. A cold-water/low-pressure pump with this capability must be verified.
- **Storage:** collected water is average g/min times EVA duration. A 90% fill fraction gives useful tank capacity. Spherical tank radius follows volume; wall thickness is the greater of 0.5 mm or `differential pressure * radius / (2 * allowable stress * joint efficiency)`. Exact shell volume gives aluminium mass; 50 g is added for ports/bladder/supports. Mars external pressure defaults to 0.6 kPa. The spherical shell equation is a preliminary membrane estimate; local stresses, bladder behavior and pressure cycling remain outside scope.
- **Shared water heat rejection:** input water is explicitly the LCG **outlet**, not its inlet. No LCG outlet-water model or shared-water flow is available in the Excel handoff, so 25 C is a clearly marked placeholder and 1.8 L/min is an editable literature reference. Pre-existing LCG heat is already represented by that incoming temperature and is not added to RH's thermal load again. External radiator area/mass/volume are excluded entirely.
- **Hot refrigerant-to-water heat exchanger:** mean refrigerant duty is margin-adjusted evaporator cooling plus mean compressor power. Physical exchanger area is sized for the larger full-capacity compressor on-state duty, using the condensing reference map. Parallel water channels are selected from a 0.3 m/s target; their Nu gives water-side h. Calculated overall resistance is `1/h_water + wall/k_aluminium + 1/h_refrigerant`. LMTD is between isothermal condensing refrigerant and incoming/outgoing water at design duty. Two broad channel faces are counted. The simplified all-wall Nu treatment, refrigerant film value, plate/header geometry and ideal heat spreading are assumptions. Short-channel entry enhancement is not credited. Friction uses the rectangular laminar Poiseuille expression plus 2 kPa header allowance. Water-channel Re >= 2300 raises an error. Metal volume conservatively treats the external refrigerant-side allowance as solid except for water passages. No refrigerant distribution or pressure-vessel qualification is established.
- **Temperatures and incremental pumping:** choose condensing temperature at least 5 K above the allowed heated-water state, and at least 100 F to remain within the compressor map. Maximum added water rise defaults to 2 K; an on-state limit check rejects inadequate flow. Approximate water properties are fixed near 25 C, suitable only for a preliminary water-loop estimate. Added shared-loop pump electrical power is `pressure drop * volume flow / 0.30`; the existing shared-loop pump's mass, volume and baseline power are outside the RH budget. The RH liquid extraction pump remains a separate component.
- **External radiator added load:** cooling duty plus RH electrical inputs, with all auxiliary electrical losses conservatively assigned to the water loop. Report both mean load and the full-capacity compressor on-state load. The actual total radiator requirement must also include LCG and other subsystem heat, which this script does not calculate. Average water temperature after RH is LCG outlet temperature plus mean RH heat divided by water heat-capacity flow. Instantaneous RH/EVA peaks and loop thermal storage still require a separate transient check.
- **Packing:** each included component has an external bounding-box volume. The spherical tank reserves a bounding cube, not just liquid volume. Sum all eight internal component boxes, including the new water heat exchanger, and divide by 0.65. No external radiator allowance is retained. No single overall box dimension is claimed without a routing/layout design.

Graphite density 1500 kg/m3 and porous ceramic density 2300 kg/m3 are **unverified bulk-density assumptions**, not claimed PG-45/ceramic measurements. They must be replaced by selected-material data before a defensible final mass budget. Cold plate dimensions, packing fraction, headers, shell gauge, accessories and margins are also explicitly marked assumptions in the input section. No oxygen compatibility is established by these sources.

## Full EVA update

The handoff now includes peak cooling, time-weighted cooling and integrated collected water. Do not calculate storage as peak moisture times EVA duration. Mean electrical power uses the full-profile mean thermal duty; installed compressor count uses peak thermal duty. Refrigerant-to-water exchanger sizing retains full installed compressor on-state heat, and water-side thermal load is reported separately for mean and on-state operation. The former 2 K rise input is an initial condensing-temperature allowance; the actual on-state water rise is calculated and a minimum hot-side approach is checked.

At the current profile the wall-transfer estimate requires 0.06617 m2, 39 channels and 247.04 W peak cooling; the 20% allowance gives 296.44 W. The component calculation is explicitly **provisional because some full-profile coil outlet states predict fog**. It does not demonstrate mist capture. Approximate package volume is 5.49 L, dry mass 3.33 kg and end-EVA mass 4.08 kg, with 0.7500 kg collected water. Mean electrical input is about 52 W; compressor on-state electrical input is about 207 W. Final values are regenerated by the scripts.

The current 65% suit-RH branch is infeasible even for an ideal infinite-area 1 C condenser at the supplied airflow and reference gas volume. Its component size is therefore reported as unavailable, rather than applying a fabricated area reduction. Review `RH_Sizing_Sources.md` for the full humidity-inventory model and assumptions. `Design Status` in the component workbook carries validity flags; the component program prints the peak limitation alongside its physical estimates.

The RH sensitivity table retains the eight +/-10% cases and adds absolute 2 C and 5 C surface cases. Their areas are 0.02701 m2 and 0.03345 m2, compared with 0.02562 m2 at 1 C. Water properties used in the thermal balance are refreshed for each surface case. These rows compare condenser area/duty; separator/drain resizing occurs when the selected main surface input is changed and the RH script is rerun.

The approximate entry-enhancement graph and area credit have been removed from RH sizing. Component sizing continues to use the fully developed baseline, with constant properties, ideal wetting and uniform wall temperature. The entry-length validity diagnostic remains; no entry-enhancement savings are claimed.


## Integrated entry point
Run `Nominal_Metabolic_Profile.m` to calculate the body profile, RH requirements and physical components in one run. The component calculations and their labelled assumptions are now in that main script. `RH_Component_Sizing.m` is a compatibility launcher for the main script. The component input table passes directly in memory; the workbook remains a saved design snapshot and is not read during component sizing.

The integration preserves the original full-EVA peak wall-transfer hardware basis, including its provisional-design warning. It does not switch component sizing to either of the two selected condenser comparison cases. Storage uses integrated EVA moisture and average refrigeration power uses time-weighted EVA cooling, as before. External radiator hardware remains excluded, with its added thermal load reported.

## Current hardware basis: 99% capacity case
Component sizing now uses row 2 of `EVA_Design.SelectedSummary`. Installed transfer area and channel count determine the condenser envelope. Its peak predicted removal determines separator area and extraction-pump capability; collected-water total determines storage. Its peak cooling duty (plus the existing margin) sizes refrigeration, and its time-weighted cooling determines average electrical demand. Straight-channel pressure loss is recalculated across the prescribed EVA inlet states for that installed channel count; the largest return flow and pressure loss are used for the blower allowance. This supersedes the previous original-peak sizing basis. Above-saturation predicted histories remain diagnostic and the component estimate is labelled provisional; the RH excursion does not stop the calculation.

## Reduced hardware inventory and equipment requirements
The current package excludes a separate condenser casing, external condenser headers, ceramic separator stack, extraction pump, and the valves/charge/tubing/controls allowance. The porous fins and two 1 mm assumed porous bases are counted at the existing graphite bulk density; liquid separation is assumed integrated within that envelope and mass. The hydraulic separator-area requirement is retained, but embedded separation construction is not dimensioned or validated. This is an integration assumption, not a demonstration that the specified hydraulic area fits a manufactured substrate.

The extraction pump has requirements only: peak liquid flow times the existing factor of two, suction plus routing pressure rise, and absolute inlet pressure. Equivalent pump duty is mean collected liquid volume flow divided by the required rated flow, assuming buffered liquid and no cycling losses. No selected pump mass, volume or electrical input is counted. Compressor equivalent duty uses mean cooling divided by installed mapped capacity; cold and hot heat-exchanger load fractions are average/design thermal-duty ratios and are not active switching schedules. Compressor electrical input and the gas-blower increment remain counted. Pump and control electrical inputs and unspecified accessory hardware are excluded; package totals therefore cover only the listed hardware and gas-blower increment. The shared-loop/external-cooling table is no longer printed; hot-side calculations remain for heat-exchanger sizing and its stated operating requirements.

## Main script organization
Editable inputs are grouped into metabolic, RH-sizing and hardware-sizing sections. Metabolic calculation, summaries and figures precede RH transfer/sensitivity/EVA sizing and physical hardware calculations. Internal component requirements pass directly from the generated input table without spreadsheet-schema checks. Unused intermediate average-state report tables and obsolete shared-loop report calculations have been removed; the reported model calculations and design basis are retained. Figures are 1 source metabolism, 2 EVA metabolism/outlet conditions, 3 moisture sources, 4 regional temperature history, 5 average airflow gradient, 6 condenser carrying capacity, 7 required area and 8 condenser EVA RH performance.
