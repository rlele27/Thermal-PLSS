# PLSS metabolic, LCG and humidity loads

Run `../Nominal_Metabolic_Profile.m` in MATLAB. Python and extra toolboxes are not required. Activity metabolic rates are mapped from `../Metabolic.xlsx` using `../Raw_Nominal.csv`. Above Nominal is 0.8 times resistance exercise. Mars conversion applies only to metabolic sizing; moisture and LCG use the Earth mapped metabolic input. No MetMan moisture calibration is applied.

## Controlled supply and neck-to-legs airflow

Run `../Nominal_Metabolic_Profile.m`. `BodyMicroclimate.AirTemperature_C` and `RelativeHumidity_percent` now specify controlled supply conditions after reheating: currently 22 C and 50% RH. Pressure remains 31.7159 kPa. Air follows three series zones: head/neck (JOS-3 segments 1-2), torso/pelvis/arms (3-11), and legs (12-17). The final leg outlet is the suit return. This is a prescribed gradient approximation, not a designed ventilation layout. Radiation uses the separately specified radiant temperature, currently 22 C.

`Ventilation.Flow_CFM` is actual flow at the supply conditions, initially 6 CFM. No suit geometry or volume is required: gas storage and transport delay are neglected compared with each 30-second body timestep. The same dry-oxygen mass flow passes through all three zones; there is no branch-flow allocation. Local skin air speed remains an independent 0.1 m/s assumption; bulk flow does not determine it without geometry.

Each timestep solves the body and steady gas heat/moisture balances together. Each region uses the mean of its inlet/outlet temperature and humidity ratio; RH is calculated at that regional temperature. Region outlets feed the next region. Respiratory sensible heat and vapor enter only the head/neck region. Every trial advances from the same starting body and retained-water state; only the converged trial is kept. Warming reduces convection; accumulating vapor reduces evaporative potential. The supplied LCG changes skin temperature and sweating through the existing body balance. Preconditioning uses the same coupling.

The ideal PLSS maintains supply conditions and removes astronaut vapor; this specifies duty, not proof of hardware capability. FOS applies once to moisture sizing, never to body physiology or physical return temperature/RH.

## Simplified LCG assumption

The main script exposes `BodyMicroclimate.LCG.ActivityCoolingFraction`. It allocates that fraction of metabolic demand above the 100 W baseline to the LCG, moderated when skin approaches the supplied water temperature. This is not a fitted physiological coefficient or measured garment effectiveness. A 0.4-0.8 sensitivity study is appropriate before relying on the resulting moisture load.

The temperature factor is the covered-area mean skin-to-water temperature difference divided by the neutral skin-to-water difference, bounded from zero to one. Contacts exclude head, neck, hands and feet and are weighted by body area. The target becomes an implicit skin heat-transfer boundary, preserving body thermal history and energy conservation. Internal LCG cooling is the actual implicit-step heat transfer, which may differ slightly from the starting-step target. The upstream system is assumed able to deliver water at the selected inlet temperature with sufficient flow and heat transfer. No garment geometry, coolant flow, contact coefficient, detailed coverage, valve controller, outlet coolant temperature or refrigeration design is required.

Water temperature and metabolism alone cannot uniquely determine real LCG performance. The allocation assumption makes that missing information explicit rather than implying garment engineering has been validated.

## All astronaut vapor sources

The PLSS humidity output includes respiratory vapor, skin diffusion, evaporation of fresh sweat and later evaporation of retained sweat. FOS is applied once to displayed moisture averages, peaks, totals and graphs. The outlet moisture output is net vapor added to the PLSS stream after air-node condensation and re-evaporation, not total sweat secretion. Astronaut vapor generation remains separately reported. LCG heat transfer remains internal solely to determine body temperature history and sweat evaporation; no LCG heat-delivery sizing table or graph is produced. Body simulation states remain unscaled.

Unevaporated sweat is retained internally by segment and can evaporate in later steps or activities. There is no retention-capacity input and no runoff calculation or collection load. All retained sweat remains available for evaporation, a conservative humidity assumption. It does not imply physical liquid can be stored without limit in an actual garment. No inventory is printed or plotted as a PLSS output. Initial retained liquid is zero before preconditioning; any accumulated water persists into the EVA.

Retained water is assumed at skin temperature, so its evaporation removes latent heat from that segment. Garment thermal storage, moisture-dependent clothing properties, wicking and suit-surface condensation are omitted. Fixed internal proxies retain 0.1 m/s local air speed, 0.2 clo and JOS-3 vapor permeation 0.45; these are not a detailed pressure-garment representation. Radiant temperature remains the input value as the ventilation gas warms. Bulk ventilation flow now participates in the coupled air balance.

## Internal balances and outputs

JOS-3 retains all 85 body temperatures. New water is sweat secretion plus skin diffusion plus respiration. The internal interval balance is `new water - vapor to suit = change in retained sweat / duration`. Retained evaporation can draw on water generated earlier. Only evaporated moisture supplies latent cooling. Nonpositive skin evaporative potential is treated as zero; condensation onto the body is not modelled.

`runJOS3Profile` retains detailed unscaled component rates and liquid inventory for internal accounting. The main script presents PLSS-relevant vapor averages/peaks/totals, without calculating or displaying a separate heat-delivered-to-PLSS summary. The first four vapor rows sum to total astronaut vapor. Net PLSS duty remains in the outlet summary and Figure 2, but its separate row and curve have been removed from the vapor-source table and Figure 3. The final physical condensate inventory is reported in the separate EVA summary. Fixed supply RH does not imply fixed return RH: the suit adds vapor before the ideal sink removes it. FOS-scaled outputs must not be balanced against unscaled simulation inventory.

Per-step water and body-energy balance assertions remain active. MATLAB checks also cover component closure, zero LCG allocation, water warmer than skin, retained-water continuity, delayed evaporation, duplicate timestamps and partial final steps. These verify implementation, not accuracy against suited human tests. The earlier detailed LCG implementation and runoff model have been replaced.

## Garment and local humidity assumptions

The 0.2 clo input is a light-garment proxy, not a measured LCG value. It gives 0.031 m2 K/W intrinsic dry resistance and, with permeability 0.45 and Lewis rate 16.5, approximately 0.00418 m2 kPa/W (4.18 m2 Pa/W) intrinsic evaporative resistance before the air layer. Thermal insulation and evaporative resistance are different properties. A light-clothing worked example in [Cramer and Jay, Partitional calorimetry](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6397408/) uses 0.3 clo and 0.010 m2 kPa/W evaporative resistance; this is a comparison point, not a measurement of this LCG.

There is no imposed `SkinAirRH_percent`. Evaporation uses calculated mixed-air vapor pressure and the existing series garment/air-film resistance from JOS-3. Local RH at the outer garment surface is estimated from the evaporative flux times air-film resistance, and its temperature from dry flux times clothing insulation. It is a diagnostic, not a second resistance applied to evaporation. `LocalGarmentRH_percent` is the area-weighted estimate limited to saturation. `MaximumGarmentRH_percent` preserves the uncapped segment maximum: above 100% indicates possible within-garment condensation, which this simplified model does not resolve. No detailed garment engineering parameters are added.

## Coupled suit return conditions

Each zone satisfies `C_supply * (T_out - T_in) = convection + respiratory sensible heat + L * phase_rate` and `m_dry * (w_out - w_in) = astronaut vapor - phase_rate`, with positive phase rate for condensation and negative for re-evaporation. Radiation and LCG heat are not added to the gas; phase-change latent heat is included explicitly. The supply mass flow follows oxygen and vapor densities, with constant specific heats of 918 and 1860 J/kg/K. Changes in vapor heat capacity along the path, duct/equipment heat, gas storage and wall condensation are neglected. Air-node saturation is resolved by liquid/vapor equilibrium with a retained liquid inventory, rather than stopping or silently discarding water. Pressure-corrected convection is used consistently in operative temperature, dry resistance and the convection/radiation split.

Zone balances are iterated with relaxation until every zone's heat residual is below 1e-6 W and water residual below 1e-7 g/min. Body-energy and retained-water checks also remain active. These verify conservation, not accuracy against human EVA data. The gradient is intentionally simplified: it represents fresh air entering near the neck and leaving at the legs without duct geometry or detailed suit engineering inputs.

The main script reports manual RH sizing inputs and moisture rates. Average and peak leg-return temperatures remain in the workspace and graph. The RH sizing report reconstructs an equivalent average return state from mean temperature and mean humidity ratio, so its RH may differ slightly from arithmetic-average RH. Its airflow is actual CFM at that return state, rather than supply CFM. The combined `Summary` table is titled Overall and suit outlet summary, with `Overall`, `SuitInlet` and `SuitOutlet` columns. SuitInlet reports the controlled supply temperature, RH, actual supply CFM and pressure. Its moisture-generation/removal rows are zero, representing no astronaut-added moisture at the supply before the air passes over the body; they do not imply dry supply gas. It includes moisture, outlet temperature, outlet RH, actual outlet CFM and pressure; Both columns now include calculated air temperature, equivalent RH and actual CFM. EVA duration, average/peak Mars-adjusted metabolic rates and final physical condensate inventory are in the separate `EVA_Summary` table. The Overall air entries are representative body-area/time-weighted values across the three regions. Published JOS-3 segment areas define the relative regional weights; they are a proxy for body exposure, not measured gas-volume weights. Temperature and humidity ratio are averaged first, then equivalent RH and actual CFM are calculated at that state. CFM is not summed across the series regions. Overall moisture remains total astronaut vapor generation, while outlet moisture is net vapor reaching the PLSS. Moisture includes the metabolic FOS. Overall moisture is astronaut vapor generation; SuitOutlet moisture is net vapor reaching the PLSS. Condensate inventory is physical and unscaled. Temperature is averaged over the final leg outlet, not over body regions. Outlet RH and CFM represent the equivalent mean outlet state reconstructed using conserved dry oxygen and the average net vapor reaching the PLSS after phase change. Metabolic Figure 6 contains separate panels for Mars-adjusted metabolic rate, suit-outlet vapor rate and suit-outlet temperature. Metabolic Figure 8 shows only the three regional mean temperatures; the separate leg-outlet trace and outlet-average guide have been removed. `Moisture_Profile` retains regional mean temperatures and RH, while `CondenserInletTemperature_C` and `CondenserInletRH_percent` refer to the final leg outlet.

## Air-node condensation and re-evaporation

Each of the three air nodes retains a separate condensate inventory, initially zero before preconditioning. It is distinct from retained sweat on the body. Liquid stays at its node: no drainage, runoff, droplet transport or storage-capacity input is introduced. Preconditioning inventories carry into the EVA and are reported as initial inventory.

At each body/air iteration, nodes are processed from neck to legs. A dry sensible balance gives provisional temperature and humidity ratio. If supersaturated, the code solves saturation and the latent-heat balance together. If liquid is available in undersaturated air, it solves evaporative cooling toward saturation, bounded by the liquid available over that timestep. The scalar root is bracketed between provisional dry temperature and dew point. Latent heat is 2418 J/g, consistent with body evaporation. Liquid sensible heat capacity and wall temperatures remain outside scope.

Only the converged body/air trial updates inventories. Condensation warms gas and re-evaporation cools it; both feed back into JOS-3. Zone-mean RH used for body feedback is bounded by saturation as a representative regional condition; actual outlet vapor flux and phase change are determined by conservative node boundary balances. This is instantaneous equilibrium at local gas temperature, not a prediction of suit-wall wetting or its finite-rate transport. Real cold walls can condense water below 100% bulk RH.

`Moisture_Profile` includes net `VaporToPLSS_g_min`, condensation and condensate-evaporation rates, latent heat, and initial/final condensate masses for every timestep and node. The physical water balance is `astronaut vapor - net vapor to PLSS = condensate inventory increase / duration`. Together with retained sweat, `new body water - net vapor to PLSS = retained-sweat increase + condensate increase` over a timestep. Assertions verify these balances and gas/body energy closure. Detailed per-node condensate remains in `Moisture_Profile`. The per-node condensate command-window table has been removed; `EVA_Summary` reports final total retained condensate.

The full eight-hour neck-supply EVA now completes with the current inputs (including 18.3 C LCG water). About 0.0571 g condenses in the leg region and later fully re-evaporates; no liquid remains at EVA end. Overall and outlet average vapor therefore match over the full EVA, although instantaneous values differ. Reported PLSS vapor duty is approximately 1.5626 g/min average and 5.1227 g/min peak including FOS. These are simplified-model outputs, not a validation against suited condensation measurements. RH sizing is included in the merged main script and uses these in-memory results.

## RH sizing literature update

The RH sizing section evaluates IAPWS liquid-water properties at its selected surface/drainage temperature, uses reported ceramic/graphite bubble pressures instead of treating average pores as maximum diameters, derives intrinsic permeability from the published hydraulic conductivity, and uses Fuller oxygen-water diffusivity. Remaining geometry, fouling and operational assumptions are explicitly labelled. See `../RH_Sizing_Sources.md` for references, interpretation limits and verification results.

RH condenser transfer now uses the Althausen paper's listed channel dimensions, a fully developed laminar heat/mass correlation and uniform 1 C wetted walls. Fixed h and overall U are removed; outlet temperature/RH and thermal cooling duty are calculated together. The channel summary reports rounding, Reynolds number and straight-channel friction, with entry effects flagged. See `../RH_Sizing_Sources.md` for assumptions and limits.

RH sizing also exports `RH_Component_Inputs.xlsx` for the separately run `RH_Component_Sizing.m`. This adds physical dimensions, packing, dry/water-loaded mass and electrical estimates using labelled assumptions and reference analogues. The sensitivity table includes 2 C and 5 C surfaces. The approximate channel-entry enhancement graph has been removed; no area reduction is credited. See `../RH_Component_Sizing_Sources.md` for references and scope.

The RH component estimate excludes external radiator hardware. It includes a refrigerant-to-water heat exchanger and reports RH-added external cooling load and water temperature rise, using an editable LCG outlet-water temperature (currently an assumed 25 C) and 1.8 L/min shared-loop flow. It does not treat the metabolic model's LCG inlet temperature as an outlet value or add LCG heat twice.
